## The Demigod's Path

### Welcome

*Read this first: who you are, how this world works, and how to use this book.*

1. **Welcome, Demigod**: checkmark
   > You remember light pouring from your hands. Now there is only dirt under your nails.
   > What: Welcome to Warbucks Chemistry. You're a demigod who has lost your power, and you'll win it back by mastering chemistry: taking matter apart into its elements and building it back up.
   > How: Follow the lines from quest to quest. Each one tells you what to do and how to do it. Click the checkmark to collect your first reward.
   > Tip: The sleeping bag skips the night without moving your spawn point.
2. **Who You Were**: checkmark *(after: Welcome, Demigod)*
   > Some things you still know without being taught: that water is two parts hydrogen to one of oxygen, that fire is a reaction, not a spirit.
   > What: Your power returns as you rebuild your understanding of matter. Every chapter of this book is one more piece of what you once were.
   > Chemistry: All matter is made of about 90 naturally occurring elements. Rearrange their atoms and you change what a thing is. That's the whole game.
   > How: Click the checkmark to continue.
3. **How Progression Works**: checkmark *(after: Who You Were)*
   > What: The pack has 8 tiers. Each one is unlocked by a chemistry milestone, not just more metal.
   > How: Fire and bronze → coke and steel → hydrogen and flight → electricity → acids → oil and plastics → silicon, PVC and the atom → antimatter.
   > Modern Industrialization's voltage tiers (LV, MV, HV, EV) are the spine. Other mods (Mekanism, AE2, Powah and more) unlock alongside them.
   > Tip: If a recipe looks different from what you remember, it's been gated on purpose. JEI always shows the real recipe.
4. **Your Toolkit**: checkmark *(after: Who You Were)*
   > What: A few mods make everything easier. Learn their keys now.
   > How: R / U over an item: recipes / uses (JEI). M: map and chunk claiming (FTB Chunks).
   > Hold ` while mining to vein mine (FTB Ultimine). Look at a block and Jade shows what it is and what it's doing.
   > Tip: Inventory sorting, crafting-grid tweaks and the trash slot are in your inventory screen; check Controls for their keys.
5. **Reading This Book**: checkmark *(after: How Progression Works)*
   > What: Every quest is written the same way, so you always know where to look.
   > How: What says what the thing is, Chemistry gives the real science, How gives the steps, and Tip saves you time.
   > Quests marked optional sit above or below the main line. They give bonus tokens but never block progress.
   > Tip: Gold lines are your memories returning. They mark the start of each chapter and its big milestones.
6. **Demigod Tokens**: checkmark *(after: Reading This Book)*
   > What: Most quests give Demigod Tokens. Spend them on convenience items by crafting: torches, food, XP, iron, coke, rubber, osmium, backpack upgrades and a Warp Stone.
   > How: Search Demigod Token in JEI and press U to see the whole shop.
   > Tip: Bigger milestones pay more tokens, and later tiers pay more than early ones. Optional quests pay too.
7. **Playing Together**: checkmark *(after: Your Toolkit)*
   > What: On a server, make a team so you share quest progress and claimed land.
   > How: /ftbteams party create <name>, then invite friends with /ftbteams party invite <player>.
   > Tip: Split the work: one player on ore processing, one on power, one exploring. Chemistry rewards a production line.
8. **Give the Game Room**: checkmark *(after: Playing Together)*
   > What: This is a large pack. Too little memory means stutters; too much can cause long pauses.
   > How: In Prism, open the instance settings and allocate about 8-10 GB. The pack's docs/README.md has the recommended Java arguments.
   > Tip: If the game hitches every few seconds, check the memory bar in F3 before blaming a mod.
9. **Begin**: checkmark *(after: Demigod Tokens, Give the Game Room)*
   > The world is waiting to be taken apart and put back together.
   > How: That's everything. Open Getting Started and take your first step.
   > Tip: You can come back to this chapter any time from the chapter list.
10. **The Periodic Table**: checkmark *(after: Who You Were)*
   > What: Chemists know 118 elements. Each one is defined by its number of protons, its atomic number.
   > Chemistry: Dmitri Mendeleev arranged the table in 1869 by repeating properties, and left gaps for elements nobody had found yet. Gallium and germanium later filled them almost exactly as he predicted.
   > Tip: The Periodic Table chapter tracks every element you collect.
11. **Nothing Is Lost**: checkmark *(after: Your Toolkit)*
   > What: Antoine Lavoisier showed in the 1780s that in a closed system, mass before a reaction equals mass after it.
   > Chemistry: Atoms are rearranged, never created or destroyed in a chemical reaction. That's why every equation balances.
   > Tip: The Dissolver and Combiner follow the same rule: what you take apart, you can build back.

### I. Fire and Bronze

*Heat drives chemistry: reduce ore with carbon, mix metals into alloys, and let steam do the work.*

1. **Pyrolysis**: 16x `minecraft:charcoal` *(after: Ready for Chemistry)*
   > You remember the first fire you ever tamed. It was smaller than this, and it frightened you more.
   > What: Charcoal is almost pure carbon: fuel, and the reducing agent that turns ore into metal.
   > Chemistry: Heat wood without enough oxygen to burn and the volatile compounds escape as smoke, tar, methanol and acetic acid. What's left is carbon. This is pyrolysis.
   > How: Smelt logs in a furnace.
   > Tip: A tree farm now saves hours later. Charcoal fuels everything until you have coke.
2. **Copper**: 16x `minecraft:copper_ingot` *(after: Ready for Chemistry)*
   > What: Copper is three quarters of bronze, and MI uses it for rotors, gears and wires.
   > Chemistry: Smelting is reduction: carbon pulls oxygen away from the metal oxide and leaves metal behind, CuO + C → Cu + CO.
   > How: Copper ore is common near the surface, especially in dripstone caves. Smelt the raw copper.
3. **Tin**: 8x `modern_industrialization:raw_tin`; 8x `modern_industrialization:tin_ingot` *(after: Copper)*
   > What: Tin is soft on its own, but a little of it makes copper much harder.
   > Chemistry: Real tin comes from cassiterite, SnO₂. Carbon reduces it the same way: SnO₂ + 2C → Sn + 2CO.
   > How: Tin ore here comes from Modern Industrialization (MI). It's grey with pale specks and fairly common underground. Mine it and smelt the raw tin.
4. **The MI Guidebook**: `modern_industrialization:guidebook` *(after: Ready for Chemistry)*
   > What: Modern Industrialization's own manual. It explains each machine and shows multiblock layouts.
   > How: 8 copper nuggets around a book. Break a copper ingot into nuggets first.
   > Tip: JEI (R/U) also shows multiblock shapes in 3D.
5. **The Forge Hammer**: `modern_industrialization:forge_hammer` *(after: Pyrolysis, Copper)*
   > What: MI's first workstation. It crushes ore and ingots into dust and hammers ingots into plates, rods and bolts.
   > How: 3 heavy weighted pressure plates over 4 iron ingots. Place it and right-click to use.
   > Tip: Ores give at most 2x here. Bigger yields come later from acids, not bigger hammers.
6. **Metal Dust**: 3x `modern_industrialization:copper_dust`; `modern_industrialization:tin_dust` *(after: The Forge Hammer, Tin)*
   > What: Solid ingots can't blend. Powders can.
   > Chemistry: Grinding a metal to powder gives it a huge surface area, so the two metals mix evenly when they melt together.
   > How: Put copper and tin ingots in the Forge Hammer: 1 ingot makes 1 dust.
7. **Mixing Bronze**: 6x `modern_industrialization:bronze_dust` *(after: Metal Dust)*
   > What: Bronze dust is the blended alloy, ready to melt into ingots.
   > Chemistry: Traditional bronze is about 88% copper and 12% tin by mass. Tin atoms jam the copper crystal so its layers can't slide, which makes it harder.
   > How: Craft 3 copper dust with 1 tin dust to get 3 bronze dust.
   > Tip: The Steam Mixer later turns the same inputs into 4 bronze dust instead of 3.
8. **The First Alloy**: 16x `modern_industrialization:bronze_ingot` *(after: Mixing Bronze)*
   > Metal that never existed until a mind decided it should. You remember the feeling.
   > What: Bronze is harder than either copper or tin alone, and it's the frame of your whole steam age.
   > Chemistry: An alloy is a mixture of metals. Its properties can beat its ingredients because the mixed atoms resist being pushed out of line.
   > How: Smelt bronze dust in any furnace.
9. **Bronze Plates**: 8x `modern_industrialization:bronze_plate` *(after: The First Alloy)*
   > What: Almost every steam machine is built from plates.
   > How: Hammer bronze ingots in the Forge Hammer. Without a hammer tool, 2 ingots make 1 plate.
   > Tip: With an Iron Hammer in the tool slot it's 1 ingot per plate, and the Steam Compressor later does it 1:1 for free.
10. **Rods, Bolts and Rings**: 2x `modern_industrialization:bronze_rod`; 4x `modern_industrialization:bronze_bolt`; `modern_industrialization:bronze_ring` *(after: Bronze Plates)*
   > What: Gears need bolts and a ring; rings need rods and bolts.
   > How: In the Forge Hammer, 1 bronze ingot makes 1 rod or 2 bolts. Craft rods and bolts into rings (check JEI).
   > Tip: Make a few extra of each. Every gear takes 4 bolts and a ring.
11. **Bronze Gears**: 2x `modern_industrialization:bronze_gear` *(after: Rods, Bolts and Rings)*
   > What: Each machine casing needs a gear at its centre.
   > How: 4 bronze plates, 4 bolts and 1 ring (check JEI for the shape).
   > Tip: Make extra rings and bolts while you're at it. You'll need several casings.
12. **Bronze Machine Casing**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_machine_casing` *(after: Bronze Gears)*
   > What: The core of every steam machine. You'll also need one for the Dissolver in Chapter II.
   > How: 8 bronze plates around 1 bronze gear.
   > Tip: Multiblock hatches each need a casing too. Make a few.
13. **Large Iron Plates**: 5x `modern_industrialization:iron_large_plate` *(after: The Forge Hammer)*
   > What: The head of the Iron Hammer is made of large plates.
   > How: Hammer iron ingots into plates (2 ingots per plate), then craft 4 plates into 1 large plate. That's about 40 iron in total.
   > Tip: Ultimine an iron vein first. This is the hungriest step of the chapter.
14. **The Iron Hammer**: `modern_industrialization:iron_hammer` *(after: Large Iron Plates)*
   > What: Put it in the Forge Hammer's tool slot to unlock better recipes: 1 ingot per plate, and crushing bricks into dust.
   > How: 5 large iron plates and 2 sticks (check JEI). The tool loses durability with each use.
   > Tip: Only use the tool slot when you need it. Plain recipes don't wear it down.
15. **Brick Dust**: 4x `modern_industrialization:brick_dust` *(after: The Iron Hammer)*
   > What: Brick dust is half of fire clay.
   > Chemistry: Potters call crushed fired clay grog. It has already shrunk in the kiln, so mixing it into raw clay stops the new brick cracking when heated.
   > How: Smelt clay balls into bricks, then hammer bricks in the Forge Hammer with the Iron Hammer in the tool slot.
16. **Fire Clay**: 6x `modern_industrialization:fire_clay_dust` *(after: Brick Dust)*
   > What: Fire clay is a refractory: a material that stays solid at temperatures that would ruin ordinary brick.
   > Chemistry: Refractory clays are rich in alumina (Al₂O₃) and silica (SiO₂), which melt far above a fire's temperature.
   > How: Craft 2 clay balls with 2 brick dust to make 3 fire clay dust.
17. **Fire Clay Bricks**: 8x `modern_industrialization:fire_clay_brick` *(after: Fire Clay)*
   > What: Single fire bricks, ready to stack into blocks.
   > Chemistry: Firing drives the bound water out of clay minerals and fuses the grains together. That change can't be undone.
   > How: Smelt fire clay dust in a furnace.
18. **Firebrick Blocks**: 3x `modern_industrialization:fire_clay_bricks` *(after: Fire Clay Bricks)*
   > What: The bronze boiler sits on a row of 3 fire clay brick blocks.
   > How: 4 fire clay bricks in a 2x2 make 1 block.
   > Tip: Chapter II's Steam Blast Furnace is built from dozens of these. Start stockpiling clay.
19. **Bronze Tank**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_tank` *(after: Bronze Machine Casing)*
   > What: A bronze tank stores fluid, and the boiler needs one to hold its water.
   > Chemistry: Bronze resists corrosion from water, which is why it was used for ship fittings for centuries.
   > How: 8 bronze plates around 1 glass block.
20. **Steam Power**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_boiler` *(after: Bronze Machine Casing, Firebrick Blocks, Bronze Tank)*
   > The engines remember you even if the world doesn't. Pressure, heat, motion: old friends.
   > What: Burn fuel, boil water, and the expanding steam does work. Welcome to the industrial revolution.
   > Chemistry: Water expands more than 1,600 times in volume when it boils into steam. That pressure is what drives the machines.
   > How: Craft it from bronze plates, a bronze tank, a furnace and 3 fire clay brick blocks. Give it water and fuel (coal or charcoal), and place steam machines next to it.
   > Tip: Steam flows into adjacent machines, or through MI fluid pipes once you have them.
21. **Steam Furnace**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_furnace` *(after: Steam Power)*
   > What: Smelts with steam instead of fuel. Your first automated machine.
   > How: Place it touching the boiler, or pipe steam to it later.
   > Tip: It smelts the same things as a vanilla furnace, so dust and raw ore can go straight in.
22. **Steam Macerator**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_macerator` *(after: Steam Power)*
   > What: Grinds ore and items into dust without wearing out a tool.
   > How: 1 ore makes 2 raw ore here (capped at 2x until acids).
   > Tip: It also grinds bricks into brick dust, so your Iron Hammer can rest.
23. **Steam Compressor**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_compressor` *(after: Steam Power)*
   > What: Presses ingots into plates 1:1, and plates into curved plates.
   > How: Place it touching the boiler and put ingots in. Curved plates come from plates you put back in.
   > Tip: Machines need lots of plates. Keep this one fed.
24. **Steam Mixer**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_mixer` *(after: Steam Power)*
   > What: Mixes dusts (like copper and tin into bronze) and fluids.
   > Chemistry: Same copper and tin, less loss: a good process wastes less of its inputs. That's called yield.
   > How: 3 copper dust + 1 tin dust makes 4 bronze dust here, one more than by hand.
25. **Steam Cutting Machine**: `modern_industrialization:bronze_cutting_machine` *(after: Steam Power)*
   > What: Cuts ingots into 2 rods, rods into bolts, and logs into 6 planks.
   > How: It needs lubricant as a cutting fluid. You'll make lubricant from creosote and redstone in Chapter II, so build it now and run it then.
   > Tip: Until then, the Forge Hammer makes rods and bolts.

### II. Steam and Steel

*Destructive distillation turns coal into coke, and coke turns iron into steel. At the end of this chapter, matter itself opens up to you.*

   > Chemistry: Firing clay drives out the water bound in its minerals and fuses the grains, turning soft mud into ceramic.

### IV. The Electric Age

*Electricity is the tool that splits compounds apart. First you have to make it, and build the circuits that use it.*

   > You remember lightning answering when you called it. Now you'll have to build it a road.
   > What: rubber sheets insulate cables and form circuit boards.
   > Chemistry: natural rubber is polyisoprene, (C₅H₈)ₙ: long chains that don't let electrons through.
   > How: check JEI (press R on Rubber Sheet) for MI's rubber recipes, from plant matter or, later, synthetic rubber.
2. **Wires and Cables**: 8x `modern_industrialization:copper_wire`; 8x `modern_industrialization:tin_cable` *(after: Steel)*
   > What: copper wire goes into every component. Tin cable carries LV power between machines.

   > Tip: tin cable is rated for LV (32 EU/t). Higher tiers need better cable.
3. **Battery Alloy**: 4x `modern_industrialization:battery_alloy_ingot` *(after: Steel)*
   > What: battery alloy makes the casing and grids of redstone batteries.
   > Chemistry: lead hardened with antimony is the grid metal of real lead-acid batteries. Pure lead is too soft.

7. **Inductor**: 2x `modern_industrialization:inductor` *(after: Wires and Cables)*

### V. Electrochemistry and Acids

*Electricity splits compounds into elements. Acids dissolve what fire can't. Ore processing becomes chemistry.*

1. **Mekanism Gases**: `mekanism:electrolytic_separator` *(after: Mekanism Circuits)*
   > You remember pulling water apart with a word. A machine and a cable will have to do.
   > What: the Electrolytic Separator makes hydrogen and oxygen as gases you can pipe and store.
   > Chemistry: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂. Current drives the reaction backwards from burning hydrogen.
   > How: feed it water and FE. Gases leave from the coloured sides (configure them with the Configurator).
2. **Pressurized Tubes**: 16x `mekanism:basic_pressurized_tube` *(after: Mekanism Gases)*
   > What: gases need sealed tubes. Item pipes and fluid pipes won't carry them.
   > How: craft basic pressurized tubes (see JEI) and run them from the separator's outputs.
   > Tip: vent what you don't need yet. A full output tank stops the whole separator.
3. **A Source of Sulfur**: 8x `modern_industrialization:sulfur_dust` *(after: Electric Macerator)*
   > What: sulfur is where sulfuric acid starts.
   > Chemistry: S is element 16. Volcanoes and fossil fuels are full of it; most industrial sulfur is stripped out of oil and gas.
   > How: macerate crushed lignite coal (about half the time you get sulfur dust), or centrifuge lava later. Press U on Sulfur Dust in JEI for all sources.
4. **Contact Process 1: SO₂**: `mekanism:chemical_oxidizer` *(after: Pressurized Tubes, A Source of Sulfur)*
   > What: the Chemical Oxidizer turns a solid into a gas. Sulfur dust becomes sulfur dioxide.
   > Chemistry: S + O₂ → SO₂. It's the sharp smell of a struck match.
   > How: one sulfur dust gives 100 mB of SO₂. Pipe it out with pressurized tubes.
5. **Contact Process 2: SO₃**: `mekanism:chemical_infuser` *(after: Contact Process 1: SO₂)*
   > What: the Chemical Infuser combines two gases. SO₂ plus oxygen from your separator gives sulfur trioxide.
   > Chemistry: 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃. Real plants pass the gases over a vanadium oxide (V₂O₅) catalyst at about 450 °C: the 'contact' in the name.
   > How: SO₂ in one side, O₂ in the other.
6. **Water Vapor**: `mekanism:rotary_condensentrator` *(after: Contact Process 2: SO₃)*
   > What: the Rotary Condensentrator converts fluids to gases and back. It turns water into water vapor.
   > How: set it to gas mode (the toggle in its GUI), pipe water in and pressurized tube out.
   > Tip: it also turns Mekanism gases into buckets, which is handy for storage and trading.
7. **Contact Process 3: H₂SO₄**: checkmark *(after: Contact Process 2: SO₃, Water Vapor)*
   > What: a second Chemical Infuser combines SO₃ with water vapor into sulfuric acid gas. Tick this when acid is flowing.
   > Chemistry: SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄. Real plants dissolve SO₃ in existing acid first (oleum), because SO₃ and water alone make a corrosive mist.
   > Tip: store the acid in a chemical tank. The 5x ore line drinks it.
8. **Salt**: 16x `modern_industrialization:salt_dust` *(after: Electric Macerator)*
   > What: salt is the start of the chlor-alkali industry: chlorine, sodium and everything made from them.
   > Chemistry: NaCl is an ionic crystal: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ held together by charge. Split it and you get a soft, reactive metal and a toxic green gas.
   > How: MI salt ore is found underground and drops salt dust.
9. **Brine**: checkmark *(after: Salt, Contact Process 1: SO₂)*
   > What: brine is salty water, and Mekanism splits it into chlorine. Tick this when you have brine.
   > How: put salt dust in a Chemical Oxidizer (15 mB brine per dust), or build a Thermal Evaporation Plant (see the side quest).
   > Tip: a second oxidizer just for salt saves you from swapping inputs.
10. **Chlorine**: checkmark *(after: Brine, Mekanism Gases)*
   > What: the Electrolytic Separator also takes brine. Out come chlorine and sodium. Tick this when chlorine is flowing.
   > Chemistry: real brine electrolysis gives Cl₂ at one electrode and H₂ + NaOH at the other. Mekanism simplifies it to sodium + chlorine.
   > Tip: use a second separator so your hydrogen and oxygen line keeps running.
11. **Hydrogen Chloride**: checkmark *(after: Chlorine, Contact Process 2: SO₃)*
   > What: hydrogen chloride drives the 4x ore line. Tick this when HCl is flowing.
   > Chemistry: H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Dissolved in water it's hydrochloric acid, the same acid in your stomach.
   > How: hydrogen in one side of a Chemical Infuser, chlorine in the other.
12. **3x Ore: Oxygen**: `mekanism:purification_chamber` *(after: Mekanism Gases, Enrichment Chamber, Crusher)*
   > What: before acids, every route caps at 2x per ore. Purifying ore with oxygen gives 3x.
   > How: ore + O₂ → 3 clumps. Crusher: clumps → dirty dust. Enrichment Chamber: dirty dust → dust.
   > Tip: Mekanism shows each step in JEI. Chain them with pipes and side configuration.
13. **4x Ore: Hydrogen Chloride**: `mekanism:chemical_injection_chamber` *(after: 3x Ore: Oxygen, Hydrogen Chloride)*
   > What: injecting hydrogen chloride splits ore into shards: 4x.

   > Tip: your 3x machines are now the back half of the 4x line. Nothing is wasted.

   > What: the Dissolution Chamber dissolves ore in sulfuric acid, giving a dirty slurry.

   > How: feed it ore and sulfuric acid gas. The slurry comes out as a chemical.

   > How: slurry in, water in, clean slurry out. An MI electric water pump keeps the water coming.

   > Chemistry: this is hydrometallurgy, the way most of the world's copper is really refined.
   > How: crystals → Injection Chamber (shards) → Purification Chamber (clumps) → Crusher → Enrichment Chamber.

### VI. Petrochemistry

*Crude oil is a mixture. Separate it by boiling point, then build polymers from the pieces.*

1. **Black Gold**: `modern_industrialization:oil_drilling_rig`; `modern_industrialization:crude_oil_bucket` *(after: MV: Aluminum)*
   > You remember the world before people: forests that grew, died and sank, pressed into oil over ages you once watched pass.
   > What: crude oil is a soup of hydrocarbons, from tiny gas molecules to long waxy chains.
   > Chemistry: hydrocarbons are just carbon and hydrogen (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ and friends). Chain length decides whether one is a gas, a fuel or a tar.
   > How: put the Oil Drilling Rig in an MI Quarry over an oil-rich area (check JEI). It slowly wears out aluminum drills.
   > Tip: pipe the crude straight into a tank. You'll need a lot of it.
2. **Fractional Distillation**: `modern_industrialization:distillation_tower` *(after: Black Gold)*
   > What: the Distillation Tower is a multiblock that splits one fluid into several fractions at once.
   > Chemistry: heat the crude and each fraction boils off at its own temperature. Light molecules rise to the top, heavy ones stay low.
   > How: the 3D layout is in JEI and the MI guidebook. Each extra layer adds one more output.
   > Tip: every other mod gets its fuel and plastic from here, so build it with room to grow.
3. **Sour Fractions**: `modern_industrialization:sulfuric_light_fuel_bucket`; `modern_industrialization:sulfuric_heavy_fuel_bucket`; `modern_industrialization:sulfuric_naphtha_bucket` *(after: Fractional Distillation)*
   > What: 1000 mB of crude gives 500 light fuel, 200 heavy fuel and 300 naphtha, but all three are 'sour'.
   > Chemistry: natural oil carries sulfur compounds. Burn them and you get SO₂, the gas behind acid rain.
   > How: run crude through the Distillation Tower and catch each output in its own tank or bucket.
4. **Sweetening the Oil**: `modern_industrialization:light_fuel_bucket`; `modern_industrialization:heavy_fuel_bucket`; `modern_industrialization:naphtha_bucket` *(after: Sour Fractions)*
   > What: hydrotreating removes the sulfur and gives clean light fuel, heavy fuel and naphtha.
   > Chemistry: hydrogen reacts with sulfur compounds to form H₂S. Real refineries turn that into sulfur and then acid; MI hands you sulfuric acid directly.
   > How: Chemical Reactor: 12 B of a sour fraction + 2 B hydrogen → 12 B clean fraction + 2 B H₂SO₄.
   > Tip: feed the acid back into your ore line. Nothing is wasted.
5. **Diesel Power**: `modern_industrialization:diesel_bucket`; `modern_industrialization:lv_diesel_generator` *(after: Sweetening the Oil)*

### VII. Materials and Nuclear

*Purity and precision: semiconductors, PVC, isotopes. Then transmutation: any element from any other.*

1. **Argon from Air**: `modern_industrialization:argon_bucket` *(after: HV: Digital Circuits)*
   > You once stirred the sky. Now you pour it, liquid, into a tank.
   > What: argon is an inert blanket gas. Growing a silicon crystal in air would ruin it.
   > Chemistry: air is 78% N₂, 21% O₂ and about 0.9% argon. Cool it until it liquefies, then separate the parts.
   > How: the Vacuum Freezer (or Pressurizer) with an Air Intake makes liquid air. The Centrifuge splits it into oxygen, nitrogen and a little argon.
   > Tip: the Heat Exchanger gives far more argon while it cools hot ingots.
2. **Semiconductor Silicon**: `modern_industrialization:monocrystalline_silicon`; 4x `modern_industrialization:silicon_wafer` *(after: Argon from Air)*
   > What: computer chips need silicon purer than 99.9999999%, grown as one single crystal.
   > Chemistry: a seed crystal is pulled slowly out of molten silicon (the Czochralski process). Every atom lines up with the one below it.
   > How: EBF: 32 silicon dust + 1 tiny iridium dust + argon → monocrystalline silicon. Cut it into wafers with lubricant.
   > Tip: iridium comes from osmium byproducts or the platinum line's residue (below).
3. **Chips**: 2x `modern_industrialization:random_access_memory`; `modern_industrialization:arithmetic_logic_unit`; `modern_industrialization:memory_management_unit` *(after: Semiconductor Silicon)*
   > What: a processing unit needs RAM, an ALU and an MMU.
   > Chemistry: a pinch of aluminum (p-type) or phosphorus (n-type) in silicon changes how it carries current. That's 'doping', the basis of every transistor.
   > How: Assembler. The ALU uses a diamond plate and logic gates, the MMU silicon wafers and platinum fine wire. Check JEI for RAM.
4. **Acetylene**: `modern_industrialization:acetylene_bucket` *(after: HV: Digital Circuits)*
   > What: acetylene (C₂H₂) has a triple bond, which makes it very reactive.
   > Chemistry: heat methane hard enough and pairs of molecules lose hydrogen and join: 2CH₄ → C₂H₂ + 3H₂.
   > How: Electric Blast Furnace: 200 mB methane → 200 mB acetylene. Cracked naphtha also gives a little.
5. **Vinyl Chloride**: `modern_industrialization:vinyl_chloride_bucket` *(after: Acetylene, Salt)*
   > Chemistry: C₂H₂ + HCl → C₂H₃Cl. HCl adds across the triple bond. This was the original industrial route to PVC.
   > How: Chemical Reactor: 1 B acetylene + 1 B hydrochloric acid → 1 B vinyl chloride.
   > Tip: your salt line's chlorine and hydrogen make the HCl.
6. **PVC**: `modern_industrialization:polyvinyl_chloride_bucket` *(after: Vinyl Chloride)*
   > What: polyvinyl chloride, (C₂H₃Cl)ₙ, is tough, fire-resistant and insulates EV circuit boards.
   > Chemistry: just like polyethylene, but every other carbon carries a chlorine atom. That's why PVC doesn't burn easily.
   > How: Chemical Reactor: 500 mB vinyl chloride + 4 tiny lead or chromium dust → 300 mB PVC.
7. **Cadmium Battery**: 4x `modern_industrialization:cadmium_dust`; 2x `modern_industrialization:cadmium_battery` *(after: HV: Digital Circuits)*
   > What: the EV hull and processing unit board each need 2 cadmium batteries.
   > Chemistry: a NiCd cell runs Cd + 2NiO(OH) + 2H₂O → Cd(OH)₂ + 2Ni(OH)₂, and it can be recharged by running it backwards.
   > How: MI centrifuges monazite dust into cadmium (in reality, it's mostly a by-product of zinc refining). Then craft the battery with annealed copper cable.
   > Tip: cadmium is toxic in real life. Here it just needs a lot of monazite.
8. **Processing Unit Board**: `modern_industrialization:processing_unit_board` *(after: PVC, Cadmium Battery)*
   > How: Assembler: digital circuit board + 4 platinum plates + 2 cadmium batteries + 8 annealed copper cable + 1500 mB PVC.
   > Chemistry: platinum never corrodes, so it makes contacts that last forever.
   > Tip: platinum comes from MI's ore processing or the Platinum Line below (check JEI).

### The Periodic Table

*Rebuild the table, group by group. Each column shares a chemistry, because each shares an outer-electron arrangement.*

1. **Reading the Table**: checkmark *(after: First Entries in the Periodic Table)*
   > You remember this chart. You used to know every box by heart.
   > What: the periodic table orders the elements by proton number. Rows are periods; columns are groups.
   > Chemistry: elements in the same group have the same number of outer (valence) electrons, so they react alike.
   > How: press U on any element in JEI to see which blocks the Dissolver turns into it.
   > Tip: this chapter is a side path. Nothing in the main story waits on it, but it pays well.
2. **Alkali Metals**: 8x `chemlib:lithium`; 8x `chemlib:sodium`; 8x `chemlib:potassium` *(after: Reading the Table)*
   > What: lithium, sodium and potassium, the softest and most reactive metals.
   > Chemistry: one valence electron, easily lost. Sodium fizzes on water (2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂); potassium burns lilac.
   > How: end stone gives lithium; lapis lazuli gives sodium (the mineral lazurite is a sodium aluminosilicate). Plants give potassium.
   > Tip: check JEI with U on each element. Many common blocks hide small amounts.
3. **Alkaline Earth Metals**: 4x `chemlib:beryllium`; 8x `chemlib:magnesium`; 8x `chemlib:calcium` *(after: Alkali Metals)*
   > What: beryllium, magnesium and calcium: lighter structural metals and the stuff of bones and limestone.
   > Chemistry: two valence electrons, so they form 2+ ions. Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃) is limestone; magnesium burns blinding white.
   > How: andesite and bone give calcium (bone via hydroxylapatite, the mineral in teeth). Beryllium and magnesium come out of their compounds: check JEI.
4. **Transition Metals**: 16x `chemlib:iron`; 16x `chemlib:copper`; 8x `chemlib:nickel`; 8x `chemlib:zinc`; 4x `chemlib:gold` *(after: Alkaline Earth Metals)*
   > What: the hard, conductive metals behind every machine you've built.
   > Chemistry: their d-orbitals fill across the row. That's why they form coloured compounds and make good catalysts.
   > How: dissolve ingots, nuggets or dusts of each metal.
   > Tip: ore you already process gives nuggets and dust. Dissolve the spares, not your ingots.
5. **Post-Transition Metals**: 8x `chemlib:aluminum`; 8x `chemlib:tin`; 8x `chemlib:lead`; 4x `chemlib:indium` *(after: Transition Metals)*
   > What: aluminum, tin, lead and indium: softer, lower-melting metals just right of the transition block.
   > Chemistry: indium tin oxide is the clear conductor in every touchscreen. Lead is dense enough to stop X-rays.
   > How: diorite dissolves into tin and indium. Aluminum and lead come from their ingots, dusts and minerals (check JEI).
6. **Liquid Metals**: 4x `chemlib:mercury`; 4x `chemlib:gallium` *(after: Transition Metals)*
   > What: mercury and gallium, the two metals you can pour (almost) at room temperature.
   > Chemistry: mercury melts at −38.8 °C; gallium at 29.8 °C, below body temperature.
   > How: ender pearls and end stone dissolve into mercury. Cobblestone gives a little gallium.
7. **Challenge: The First Twenty**: `chemlib:hydrogen`; `chemlib:helium`; `chemlib:lithium`; `chemlib:beryllium`; `chemlib:boron`; `chemlib:carbon`; `chemlib:nitrogen`; `chemlib:oxygen`; `chemlib:fluorine`; `chemlib:neon`; `chemlib:sodium`; `chemlib:magnesium`; `chemlib:aluminum`; `chemlib:silicon`; `chemlib:phosphorus`; `chemlib:sulfur`; `chemlib:chlorine`; `chemlib:argon`; `chemlib:potassium`; `chemlib:calcium` *(after: Noble Gases)*
   > What: one of each of elements 1 to 20, the four shortest rows of the table.
   > Chemistry: these twenty make up almost all of your body, the oceans and the air.
   > How: most come from the groups in this chapter. Boron and the noble gases need the Fusion Chamber (boron is 5: try H + Be).
   > Tip: an optional collection. Keep one of each in a chest as you go.
8. **The Coinage Metals**: 8x `chemlib:copper`; 8x `chemlib:silver`; 8x `chemlib:gold` *(after: Transition Metals)*
   > What: the three metals humans have minted into money for thousands of years.
   > Chemistry: they're unreactive enough to keep their shine. Silver is the best electrical conductor of any element.
   > How: deepslate dissolves into a little silver. Check JEI for richer sources.
9. **Metalloids**: 4x `chemlib:germanium`; 16x `chemlib:silicon`; 4x `chemlib:arsenic` *(after: Reading the Table)*
   > What: germanium, silicon and arsenic sit on the staircase between metals and non-metals.
   > Chemistry: silicon conducts a little, and a trace of arsenic (doping) controls how much. The first transistor (1947) was germanium.
   > How: sand dissolves into silicon dioxide, which dissolves again into silicon and oxygen. Blaze powder and Nether bricks give germanium.
   > Tip: you'll need a lot of silicon later, for circuits and AE2.
10. **Non-Metals**: 16x `chemlib:nitrogen`; 8x `chemlib:phosphorus`; 16x `chemlib:sulfur` *(after: Metalloids)*
   > What: nitrogen, phosphorus and sulfur. Life and industry both run on them.
   > Chemistry: nitrogen and phosphorus feed every crop; sulfur becomes sulfuric acid, the most-made chemical on Earth.
   > How: check JEI with U. Nether blocks are a good place to look for sulfur.
11. **Chalcogens**: 16x `chemlib:oxygen`; 16x `chemlib:sulfur`; 4x `chemlib:selenium` *(after: Non-Metals)*
   > What: oxygen, sulfur and selenium. 'Chalcogen' means 'ore-former'.
   > Chemistry: two electrons short of a full shell, so they grab metals: most ores are oxides or sulfides (Fe₂O₃, ZnS, PbS).
   > How: fish and crimson fungus dissolve into selenium. Oxygen comes from nearly everything.
   > Tip: selenium is a trace nutrient: you need a little, and too much is poisonous.
12. **Halogens**: 4x `chemlib:fluorine`; 8x `chemlib:chlorine`; 4x `chemlib:bromine`; 4x `chemlib:iodine` *(after: Chalcogens)*
   > What: fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine, the most reactive non-metals.
   > Chemistry: one electron short of full. 'Halogen' means 'salt-maker': Na + Cl → NaCl, table salt.
   > How: magma blocks give fluorine and bromine. Horn coral gives iodine (via lead iodide). Chlorine comes out of salts: check JEI.
13. **Noble Gases**: 8x `chemlib:helium`; 8x `chemlib:neon`; 8x `chemlib:argon` *(after: Halogens, Post-Transition Metals, EV: Processing Units)*
   > Two halves of the table, joined. More of the old knowledge settles into place.
   > What: helium, neon and argon, the gases that refuse to react.
   > Chemistry: a full outer shell means nothing to gain or lose. Argon shields welds from air; neon glows red in a discharge tube.
   > How: nothing dissolves into them, so build them: the Alchemistry Fusion Chamber (EV) adds proton numbers, so H + H → He. Check JEI for neon and argon.
   > Tip: real stars fuse hydrogen into helium too, though it takes them four protons, not two.
14. **Heavy Noble Gases**: 2x `chemlib:xenon`; 2x `chemlib:radon` *(after: Post-Transition Metals)*
   > What: xenon and radon, the heaviest natural noble gases.
   > Chemistry: in 1962 xenon was made to react (with platinum hexafluoride), ending the idea that noble gases never react. Radon is radioactive.
   > How: dragon's breath dissolves into both. That means a trip to the End.
15. **Rare Earths**: 4x `chemlib:lanthanum`; 4x `chemlib:cerium`; 4x `chemlib:neodymium` *(after: Noble Gases, EV: Processing Units)*
   > What: the lanthanides: lanthanum, cerium, neodymium and their near-identical neighbours.
   > Chemistry: they differ only in an inner (4f) shell, so their chemistry is almost the same. Neodymium makes the strongest permanent magnets.
   > How: check JEI for sources. The Alchemistry Fusion Chamber can also build them from lighter elements.
16. **Actinides**: 4x `chemlib:thorium`; 8x `chemlib:uranium`; 4x `chemlib:plutonium` *(after: Noble Gases, Transmutation by Neutron)*
   > What: thorium, uranium and plutonium, the fuels of the nuclear age.
   > Chemistry: every actinide is radioactive. Plutonium barely exists in nature: you made it by firing neutrons at uranium.
   > How: dissolve uranium and thorium materials. Plutonium comes from your fission reactor's waste.
17. **Isotopes: Heavy Water**: `mekanism:electric_pump`; `mekanism:upgrade_filter`; `mekanism:heavy_water_bucket` *(after: Alkali Metals, Mekanism: Metallurgic Infuser)*
   > What: heavy water, D₂O: water made with deuterium, hydrogen with an extra neutron.
   > Chemistry: isotopes have the same protons (so the same chemistry) but different neutrons. About 1 in 6,400 hydrogen atoms is deuterium.
   > How: put a Filter Upgrade in a Mekanism Electric Pump and pump from water. It pulls out heavy water instead.
   > Tip: deuterium is half of fusion fuel. You'll want this again in the Power chapter.
18. **Superheavy Elements**: `chemlib:tennessine` *(after: Rare Earths, Actinides)*
   > What: tennessine, element 117. Nothing in the world dissolves into it; you have to build it.
   > Chemistry: real labs make superheavy elements by smashing nuclei together; only a handful of atoms have ever existed.
   > How: the Alchemistry Fusion Chamber joins two elements, adding their proton numbers. Check JEI for the pairs that reach 117.
   > Tip: one more step after this: add hydrogen (1) to tennessine (117) and you get 118.
19. **Element 118**: `chemlib:oganesson` *(after: Superheavy Elements, The Complete Periodic Table)*
   > The last box on the table. You remember now: you once named these.
   > What: oganesson, the heaviest element ever made.
   > Chemistry: in reality it lasts under a millisecond. Here it sits in your inventory, which is the kind of thing demigods can do.
   > How: fuse tennessine with hydrogen (or any pair adding to 118) in the Fusion Chamber.
20. **Covalent Bonds: CO₂**: 4x `chemlib:carbon_dioxide` *(after: Non-Metals)*
   > What: carbon dioxide, your first compound in this chapter.
   > Chemistry: C + O₂ → CO₂. Non-metals don't hand electrons over; they share them in covalent bonds (O=C=O).
   > How: in the Combiner, pick Carbon Dioxide and supply 1 carbon + 2 oxygen.
   > Tip: the Combiner GUI shows each recipe once you select its output.
21. **Hydrocarbons**: 4x `chemlib:methane`; 4x `chemlib:ethylene` *(after: Covalent Bonds: CO₂)*
   > What: methane (natural gas) and ethylene (the building block of polyethylene).
   > Chemistry: CH₄ has four single bonds. C₂H₄ has a C=C double bond, which is what lets it link into plastic chains.
   > How: Combiner: methane = 1 C + 4 H; ethylene = 2 C + 4 H.
22. **Ammonia**: 4x `chemlib:ammonia` *(after: Hydrocarbons)*
   > What: ammonia, NH₃, the base of nearly all fertiliser.
   > Chemistry: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. Breaking nitrogen's triple bond is hard; the Haber-Bosch process needs heat, pressure and an iron catalyst.
   > How: Combiner: 1 nitrogen + 3 hydrogen. Later you'll run real Haber-Bosch in an MI machine.
23. **Ionic Bonds: Salt**: 8x `chemlib:sodium_chloride` *(after: Ammonia, Halogens)*
   > What: sodium chloride, NaCl: table salt.
   > Chemistry: sodium gives its one electron to chlorine. Na⁺ and Cl⁻ then hold together in a cubic crystal: an ionic bond.
   > How: Combiner: 1 sodium + 1 chlorine.
   > Tip: a reactive metal plus a poisonous gas makes something you sprinkle on food. Chemistry is strange.
24. **Strong Acids**: 4x `chemlib:hydrochloric_acid`; 4x `chemlib:nitric_acid` *(after: Ionic Bonds: Salt)*
   > What: hydrochloric acid (HCl) and nitric acid (HNO₃).
   > Chemistry: acids give up H⁺ ions in water. Mixed 3:1, HCl and HNO₃ make aqua regia, which dissolves gold and platinum.
   > How: Combiner: HCl = 1 H + 1 Cl; HNO₃ = 1 H + 1 N + 3 O.
25. **King of Chemicals**: 8x `chemlib:sulfuric_acid` *(after: Strong Acids)*
   > Built atom by atom. The old craft is coming back faster now.
   > What: sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, built straight from elements.
   > Chemistry: industry makes it by the contact process: S → SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄. Here you skip the plant and place the atoms yourself.
   > How: Combiner: 2 hydrogen + 1 sulfur + 4 oxygen.
26. **Sugar and Wood**: 2x `chemlib:sucrose`; 2x `chemlib:cellulose` *(after: Covalent Bonds: CO₂)*
   > What: sucrose (table sugar) and cellulose (what wood and paper are made of).
   > Chemistry: plants make both from CO₂ and water using sunlight. Cellulose is a chain of glucose rings: C₆H₁₀O₅ per unit.
   > How: Combiner: sucrose = 12 C + 22 H + 11 O; cellulose = 6 C + 10 H + 5 O.
   > Tip: that's a lot of atoms. Stock up on carbon, hydrogen and oxygen first.
27. **Alcohol**: 4x `chemlib:hydroxide`; 2x `chemlib:ethanol` *(after: Hydrocarbons)*
   > What: ethanol, C₂H₅OH, made by attaching a hydroxide (OH) group to a two-carbon chain.
   > Chemistry: the -OH group is what makes it an alcohol and lets it mix with water.
   > How: Combiner: hydroxide = 1 O + 1 H, then ethanol = 2 C + 5 H + 1 hydroxide.
28. **Lye**: 4x `chemlib:sodium_hydroxide` *(after: Ionic Bonds: Salt)*
   > What: sodium hydroxide, NaOH: lye, a strong base.
   > Chemistry: industry makes it by electrolysing salt water: 2NaCl + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + Cl₂ + H₂. Acid + base → salt + water.
   > How: Combiner: 1 sodium + 1 hydroxide (make hydroxide from 1 O + 1 H).
29. **Rust**: 4x `chemlib:iron_oxide` *(after: Strong Acids)*
   > What: iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃: rust, and also hematite, the most common iron ore.
   > Chemistry: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃. A blast furnace runs this backwards, using carbon to pull the oxygen off.
   > How: Combiner: 2 iron + 3 oxygen.

### Storage and Logistics

*A god needs somewhere to keep things.*

1. **Sophisticated Barrel**: `sophisticatedstorage:barrel` *(after: A Backpack)*
   > You remember vaults that held the stars. A barrel is humbler, but it's a start.
   > What: Sophisticated Storage chests and barrels take upgrades, just like your backpack. Sophisticated Storage Plus raises their limits.
   > How: craft a wooden barrel (check JEI). Tier upgrades improve it in place later.
   > Tip: barrels keep their contents when broken, so moving a full one is easy.
2. **Tier Upgrades**: `sophisticatedstorage:basic_to_iron_tier_upgrade` *(after: Sophisticated Barrel)*
   > What: tier upgrades turn a placed chest or barrel into the next tier (copper, iron, gold, diamond, netherite) without moving items.
   > How: craft the upgrade (check JEI) and right-click it on the placed container.
   > Tip: you can skip tiers with the direct upgrades, like basic → iron.
3. **Compacting**: `sophisticatedstorage:compacting_upgrade` *(after: Tier Upgrades)*
   > What: the Compacting Upgrade squeezes 9 nuggets into an ingot and 9 ingots into a block as items arrive.
   > Chemistry: a block holds the same metal atoms as 9 ingots; only the packing changes, so no mass is lost either way.
   > How: craft it (check JEI) and put it in an upgrade slot. Add a filter so it only compacts what you choose.
4. **Storage Controller**: `sophisticatedstorage:controller`; 2x `sophisticatedstorage:storage_link` *(after: Compacting)*
   > What: the Controller joins touching Sophisticated containers into one inventory; Storage Links extend its reach.
   > How: place it next to your chests and barrels. Pipes into the controller reach every connected container.
   > Tip: one pipe into the controller is a simple sorting system: items go where matching items already are.
5. **Drawers**: `functionalstorage:oak_1` *(after: Sophisticated Barrel)*
   > What: Functional Storage drawers hold lots of a single item and show it on the front.
   > How: craft an oak drawer (1, 2 or 4 slots; check JEI). Right-click to insert, left-click to take.
   > Tip: upgrades (copper, gold, diamond, netherite) multiply drawer capacity.
6. **Compacting Drawer**: `functionalstorage:compacting_drawer` *(after: Drawers)*
   > What: a compacting drawer stores nuggets, ingots and blocks of one metal as a single pool, and hands out whichever you ask for.
   > How: craft it (check JEI), then insert the item you want to track.
   > Tip: perfect for copper and tin near your bronze setup.
7. **Drawer Controller**: `functionalstorage:storage_controller` *(after: Compacting Drawer)*
   > What: the Storage Controller links nearby drawers into one inventory you (and pipes) can use.
   > How: place it among your drawers. Use the Linking Tool to add drawers further away.
   > Tip: a Controller Extension gives you a second access point.
8. **An Early Network**: `toms_storage:storage_terminal`; `toms_storage:inventory_connector` *(after: Drawers)*
   > What: Tom's Simple Storage connects your chests into one searchable terminal. It's your network until AE2.
   > How: place an Inventory Connector touching your chests (or link them with Inventory Cables), and a Storage Terminal on it.
   > Tip: point it at a drawer controller and every drawer shows up in the terminal.
9. **Crafting Terminal**: `toms_storage:crafting_terminal` *(after: An Early Network)*
   > What: a terminal with a crafting grid that pulls ingredients from the network. JEI's + button fills it for you.
   > How: upgrade a Storage Terminal (check JEI).
   > Tip: a Wireless Terminal (also in JEI) lets you use it from across the base.
10. **Faster Furnaces**: `ironfurnaces:iron_furnace` *(after: Iron)*
   > What: Iron Furnaces are faster furnaces in metal tiers. A generator augment turns one into a small FE source.
   > Chemistry: smelting iron ore is a reduction: carbon pulls the oxygen off, Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.
   > How: ring a furnace with iron (check JEI). Upgrade kits move it up a tier without breaking it.
11. **Pipez**: 8x `pipez:item_pipe` *(after: Iron)*
   > What: simple pipes for moving items, fluids and energy between non-MI machines.
   > How: place a line from source to target, then set the end touching the source to extract (the pipe tooltip explains how).
   > Tip: MI machines have their own pipes; Pipez is best for everything else.
12. **LaserIO**: 2x `laserio:laser_node` *(after: Pipez, LV Machine Hull)*
   > What: Laser Nodes move items, fluids and energy between machines without cables.
   > How: place nodes on machines, link them with the Laser Wrench, and add cards to set what moves (check JEI).
   > Tip: one node can serve all six faces of a block, great for compact chemical setups.
13. **AE2 Storage**: `ae2:drive`; `ae2:terminal` *(after: The God's Archive)*
   > You remember a mind that held every name of every thing. The archive is waking.
   > What: ME Drives hold storage cells; terminals let you see and search everything.
   > How: connect the drive and a terminal to your ME Controller with cables (check JEI).
   > Tip: keep power stable: an Energy Cell buffers the network.

   > What: cells are where the network actually stores things. Bigger components (4k, 16k, 64k, 256k) hold more.
   > Chemistry: certus and fluix are fictional, but the logic processors behind them echo real chips: doped silicon (Si).
   > How: craft components from processors and certus quartz, then put one in a cell housing (check JEI).
15. **Autocrafting**: `ae2:crafting_terminal` *(after: Storage Cells)*
   > What: pattern providers plus molecular assemblers turn whole chemical chains into one click.
   > How: a Crafting Terminal, some crafting storage, and patterns encoded in a Pattern Encoding Terminal (check JEI).
   > Tip: processing patterns can drive MI machines too: send the inputs, wait for the outputs.
16. **ExtendedAE**: `extendedae:ex_pattern_provider`; `extendedae:ex_drive` *(after: Autocrafting)*
   > What: ExtendedAE adds larger versions of AE2 blocks: the Extended Pattern Provider holds many more patterns, the Extended Drive many more cells.

   > Tip: one extended provider can feed a whole row of chemical reactors.

   > What: MEGA Cells adds 1M to 256M cells, bulk cells and bigger crafting storage.

   > What: Applied Mekanistics lets AE2 store and move Mekanism chemicals: H₂, O₂, Cl₂, HCl, SO₂ and the rest.
   > Chemistry: a 1k cell holds a lot of gas because the network stores amounts, not tanks. Real gases need pressure vessels.

   > What: Applied Flux stores FE in AE2 cells and moves it through the network with Flux Accessors.

20. **Wireless Access**: `ae2:wireless_crafting_terminal` *(after: AE2 Storage)*
   > What: reach your ME network from anywhere in range of a Wireless Access Point.
   > How: check JEI. AE2 Wireless Terminals adds a universal terminal; the Infinity Booster Card removes the range limit.

   > What: the ME Requester keeps a set amount of an item in stock by ordering crafts automatically.

### Farming

*Grow what you need, within the rules of chemistry.*

1. **Knife and Board**: `farmersdelight:flint_knife`; `farmersdelight:cutting_board` *(after: Something to Eat)*
   > You remember feasts that lasted a week. Start with something smaller.
   > What: Farmer's Delight adds crops, meals and kitchen blocks. The knife harvests straw and cuts ingredients.
   > How: craft a Flint Knife and a Cutting Board. Place items on the board and use the knife on them.
   > Tip: a knife on tall grass gives straw; wild crops (tomatoes, onions, cabbage, rice) grow in the wild.
2. **Cooking Pot**: `farmersdelight:cooking_pot` *(after: Knife and Board)*
   > What: the Cooking Pot turns several ingredients into one meal. It needs heat underneath.
   > Chemistry: browning meat is the Maillard reaction: amino acids and sugars react above about 140 °C, making hundreds of flavour compounds.
   > How: iron, bricks, a wooden shovel and a water bucket (check JEI). Place it over a fire, campfire or Stove.
3. **A Proper Meal**: 2x `farmersdelight:vegetable_soup` *(after: Cooking Pot)*
   > What: Farmer's Delight meals restore a lot of hunger and give long effects like Nourishment (hunger stops dropping) and Comfort.
   > How: cook vegetable soup in the Cooking Pot (check JEI), then bowl it.
   > Tip: a Feeding Upgrade in your backpack eats these for you.
4. **Rich Soil**: `farmersdelight:organic_compost`; 4x `farmersdelight:rich_soil` *(after: Knife and Board)*
   > What: organic compost slowly turns into Rich Soil, which grows crops faster and mushrooms into colonies.
   > Chemistry: composting is microbes breaking down organic matter with oxygen. They release CO₂ and heat, and leave humus rich in nitrogen.
   > How: craft compost from dirt, straw and bone meal plus rotten flesh or tree bark (check JEI), place it and wait.
5. **A Feast**: `farmersdelight:honey_glazed_ham_block` *(after: A Proper Meal)*
   > What: feasts are placed as blocks and served with a bowl, several portions each.
   > How: cook a Honey Glazed Ham (check JEI). Place it on a table and right-click with bowls.
   > Tip: a feast is the easiest way to feed a whole team before a boss fight.
6. **Hopper Botany Pots**: `botanypots:terracotta_hopper_botany_pot` *(after: Something to Eat)*
   > What: Hopper Botany Pots grow crops and push the harvest into the container below.
   > How: place one on a chest, add soil (dirt) and a seed.
   > Tip: Rich Soil or farmland grows faster than dirt, and Mystical Agriculture seeds work in pots too.
7. **Mystical Agriculture**: `mysticalagriculture:infusion_altar`; 16x `mysticalagriculture:inferium_essence` *(after: Hopper Botany Pots)*
   > What: resource crops grow essence that crafts into real materials. Seeds are made on the Infusion Altar with Infusion Pedestals around it.
   > How: inferium ore gives inferium essence. Craft the altar and pedestals (check JEI).
   > Tip: Tier 3/4/5 seeds need MI LV/MV/HV circuits, and crops for chemistry milestones (uranium, osmium, fluorite, silicon, steel, aluminum and others) can't be grown. Farming is a convenience here, not a shortcut.
8. **Prosperity**: 8x `mysticalagriculture:prosperity_shard`; `mysticalagriculture:prosperity_seed_base` *(after: Mystical Agriculture)*
   > What: prosperity shards come from prosperity ore. They make seed bases (the core of every resource seed) and infusion crystals.
   > How: mine prosperity ore; 4 shards around wheat seeds make a Prosperity Seed Base.
   > Tip: Ultimine the veins; you'll want a lot of shards.
9. **Infusion Crystal**: `mysticalagriculture:infusion_crystal` *(after: Prosperity)*
   > What: a reusable crystal that combines lower essence into higher tiers.
   > How: a diamond, prosperity shards and inferium essence.
   > Tip: the Master Infusion Crystal never wears out; worth it once you have spare diamonds.
10. **Prudentium**: 8x `mysticalagriculture:prudentium_essence` *(after: Infusion Crystal)*
   > What: the second essence tier, for tier 2 seeds (JEI lists every crop and its tier).
   > How: 4 inferium essence around an infusion crystal make 1 prudentium (check JEI).
   > Tip: it uncrafts back to inferium if you overshoot (check JEI).

   > What: tier 3 seeds (iron, copper, redstone, nether quartz, tin, lead and more) need Tertium essence and an MI analog circuit.
   > Chemistry: the analog circuit stands on steel, and steel stands on coke: coal baked without air until only carbon is left.
   > How: 4 prudentium around an infusion crystal make 1 tertium. Each seed type costs its circuit once; planted seeds multiply.
